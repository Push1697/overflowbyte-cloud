### How NYSE moved to Linux and Reduced infra cost.

In the fast-paced world of global finance, the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) stands as an iconic symbol of capitalism and financial power. For over two centuries, it has driven the pulse of the global economy, adapting and evolving with every passing decade. But behind the scenes of this historic institution, an often-overlooked technological revolution has taken place — one that has quietly transformed how the NYSE operates at its core.

The story begins in 2007, when the NYSE embarked on a bold transformation by embracing a technology that, until then, was primarily associated with developers and smaller enterprises: **Linux**. The decision wasn’t made lightly. The NYSE had, up until that point, relied on proprietary platforms such as HP-UX, IBM AIX, and Sun Solaris to power its mission-critical systems. These systems managed billions of trades, processing massive amounts of financial data with absolute precision. But as the demands of the global financial market grew and became more interconnected, the NYSE recognized it needed a more **flexible**, **cost-effective**, and **scalable **solution.

That solution arrived with the acquisition of **Euronext**, a European stock exchange. This merger marked a turning point. The NYSE needed to unify its systems across both U.S. and European markets, and proprietary systems were no longer enough to handle the global scale required. Enter **Red Hat Enterprise Linux (RHEL)**, a technology that would revolutionize the way the world’s largest stock exchange conducted its business.

### The Evolution: Why Linux?

The decision to adopt Linux was not just a shift in software — it was a strategic leap into the future. For NYSE, Linux offered a level of **flexibility** that its previous systems could not. Unlike proprietary platforms that tied the exchange to specific vendors and expensive hardware, Linux provided a **technology-independent** solution that could run on commodity servers. This meant cost savings, scalability, and the freedom to innovate without being held back by high licensing fees or restrictive contracts.

Red Hat’s **enterprise-grade support** was another key factor. In a world where milliseconds can make or break a trade, the NYSE needed a system it could rely on. Red Hat provided the NYSE with **round-the-clock technical support**, ensuring that any potential issues could be resolved quickly before they affected trading operations.

By 2007, **Linux was everywhere at NYSE**, running not only the secondary systems but also the **mission-critical trading platforms**. Steve Rubinow, the NYSE’s Chief Information Officer at the time, said it best when he described Red Hat as “like water; it’s pervasive within our architecture.” This was no exaggeration — without Linux, Rubinow explained, most of the exchange’s systems wouldn’t be running.

### A New Era of Performance

Underneath the impressive trading floors of the **NYSE**, a powerful system of servers and processors hums away — processing **millions of quotes and trades** every second. In the past, these systems relied on **proprietary hardware**, but the shift to Red Hat Enterprise Linux allowed the NYSE to run on a more cost-effective infrastructure. Instead of expensive, high-end machines, the NYSE turned to [HP ProLiant servers](https://www.hpe.com/in/en/hpe-proliant-servers.html) — over **200 four-way HP **[ProLiant DL585s](https://support.hpe.com/hpesc/public/docDisplay?docId=c02287358&docLocale=en_US) and [400 ProLiant BL 685c blade servers](https://www.hpe.com/psnow/doc/c04140692), all running [RHEL](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6I1gFDgd268).

This switch enabled the NYSE to achieve **high performance on commodity hardware**, significantly reducing operational costs while maintaining the **robust processing power** needed to keep the financial engine running smoothly.

### The Power of Open Source in a High-Stakes Environment

Linux’s strengths go beyond just cost-efficiency. For the NYSE, **reliability and security** were critical. The financial sector is one of the most highly regulated and scrutinized industries in the world, and any breach of security could result in devastating consequences. Linux, known for its **rock-solid security reputation**, offered a variety of features that protected the NYSE’s trading systems from cyber threats. **SELinux (Security-Enhanced Linux)**, an advanced security module integrated into RHEL, provided the exchange with the peace of mind that its data was secure.

Moreover, the **scalability** of Linux was crucial in allowing the NYSE to expand and adapt to the needs of the global markets. The NYSE handles a vast range of financial products — **equities, bonds, options, derivatives, and more** — in both the U.S. and across **five European countries**. Linux’s flexibility allowed the NYSE to scale its operations across multiple continents, ensuring a seamless and unified trading experience.

### The Impact on Financial Markets

Today, **NYSE Euronext**, the global trading powerhouse that resulted from the NYSE-Euronext merger, runs on Red Hat Enterprise Linux, powering **high-frequency trading systems**, **market data processing**, and **risk management platforms**. Red Hat’s stability has ensured that the NYSE maintains **near-continuous uptime**, a critical factor for the stock exchange, where even a few seconds of downtime could result in financial disaster.

Linux’s ability to handle **real-time processing** ensures that the exchange’s trading platforms remain responsive, even during periods of extremely high trade volumes. This reliability not only ensures the smooth functioning of markets but also builds trust with traders and investors worldwide.

### A Lasting Legacy

The adoption of Linux by the NYSE represents more than just a technological shift — it symbolizes the growing dominance of **open-source software** in industries where proprietary systems once reigned supreme. What began as a tool for developers has evolved into a **mission-critical platform** for the world’s largest financial markets.

Today, the NYSE stands as a testament to the power of **innovation, open-source technology, and forward-thinking leadership**. The decision to adopt Red Hat Enterprise Linux wasn’t just about cutting costs or upgrading hardware — it was about positioning the **NYSE **to thrive in the rapidly evolving world of global finance.

As the financial landscape continues to change, one thing is certain: Linux will remain at the heart of the **NYSE’s **systems, quietly powering the trades and transactions that keep the global economy moving.
